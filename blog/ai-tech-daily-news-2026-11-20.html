AI & Tech Daily News, November 20, 2026

A busy day landed across hardware, security, and AI policy. Raspberry Pi published a Q&A with the engineers who design the boards. OpenSSL patched a critical buffer overflow. Synology issued an advisory. The UK admitted it served Apple a secret encryption order. I have the full roundup below, with every source linked.

Raspberry Pi engineers open up

The Raspberry Pi Foundation published an Ask an Engineer Q&A with the people who actually design the boards. It is a rare peek behind the curtain, and I found it worth reading even if you already own a Pi. They talk about their work and what is next for the ecosystem. You can read it here.

OpenSSL patches a critical buffer overflow

OpenSSL patched a high severity heap overflow in its Base64 encoder. The flaw is tracked as CVE-2026-121481 with a CVSS score of 9.8, and the fix landed in pull request 33652. This is the kind of vulnerability that deserves attention quickly. If you run OpenSSL anywhere, check your builds and plan the patch. The fix is available here.

Synology security advisory

Synology published security advisory SA_26_21. If you run a Synology NAS or router, check the advisory and apply any applicable updates. I do not enjoy chasing NAS updates on a Friday morning, but it beats explaining a compromised backup later. The advisory is at Synology's site.

Linux 7.5 may lock down /proc/kcore

A queued patch for Linux 7.5 lets distributions disable /proc/kcore at boot. That blocks attackers from reading kernel memory through that interface. It is opt-in for now, but the direction is clear: production kernels are moving toward more hardening by default. Phoronix covered the patch here.

AI weapons detection misfires again

Ars Technica reports on a school AI weapons-detection system that produced over 99 percent false positives. One alarm was triggered by a Doritos bag. The lawsuit alleges the vendor knew the false positive rate and marketed the system anyway. This is a cautionary tale for anyone buying AI safety tools without testing them first. The full story is here.

Onlook gives designers a visual React editor

Launch HN featured Onlook, a Y Combinator W25 project that lets designers edit React components visually and sync changes back to code. The repository is MIT-licensed and gaining traction. It is a nice capture for the developer tool kit. You can find it here.

UK admits secret encryption order to Apple

Following a tribunal ruling, the UK government confirmed it served Apple a Technical Capability Notice under the Investigatory Powers Act. The notice demanded backdoor access to encrypted iCloud data. Apple has not complied publicly. The implications reach into iOS sideloading and end-to-end encryption generally. MacAnorak reported the admission here.

Pete Warden on local AI and voice interfaces

The Data Exchange published an interview with Pete Warden, known for TensorFlow Lite and Useful Sensors. He covers running LLMs on microcontrollers, the Moonshine speech model, and why he thinks the next wave of AI lives on-device. Essential listening for Pi and edge AI tinkerers. The interview is here.

Today's mix runs from critical security patches to AI policy, with a few fun hardware stories in between. The OpenSSL fix and the Synology advisory deserve the fastest response. Everything else is worth a look when you have a few minutes.
